Grievance Redress Mechanism

Even the best advice sometimes goes wrong — and when it does, the investor needs a clear, reliable route to a remedy. This chapter maps that route across the whole financial system. The pattern is remarkably consistent: always approach the product or service provider first; only if unresolved does the grievance climb to the industry regulator, then to an ombudsman or tribunal, and finally to the courts. You'll learn what makes a grievance system robust, how SCORES and the ODR portal work for the capital market, and the parallel machinery in banking (BCSBI, the Integrated Ombudsman, CPGRAMS), insurance (the Insurance Ombudsman, IGMS) and pensions (NPS, the CRA and the NPS Trust) — closing with the Securities Appellate Tribunal and the other fora.

Consumer protection & the investor grievance mechanism

The Consumer Protection Act, 1986 was enacted to provide for better protection of the interests of consumers, and to establish consumer councils and other authorities for the settlement of consumer disputes. A consumer is a person who buys goods or hires/avails services for a consideration — but the definition excludes anyone who obtains goods/services for resale or for a commercial purpose.

What makes a grievance redress system robust

A well-designed system is defined by seven features. Together they ensure the right complaints are received, tracked and closed within time — building investor confidence. Tap each to see what it does:

The seven features — tap one

A robust system controls what comes in, records it, filters it, routes it, tracks it and closes it — with a way to escalate.
Source of receipt of complaint
Ensures complaints arise from relevant issues covered under the SEBI Act, SCRA, Depositories Act and relevant provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 — so the right complaints are addressed.
Date & time of receipt
Complaints must be received within a specific time period per the relevant acts, so the system isn't clogged with old, hard-to-dispose complaints.
Nature of complaint
Only relevant complaints are addressed, eliminating frivolous ones and keeping the system efficient.
Internal system vs escalation
The complaint first lands in the internal system of the entity complained against — a chance to resolve at the source — before escalation to an outside agency.
Status of resolution / action taken
The investor sees progress via an Action Taken Report (ATR), or is told the complaint is resolved — raising confidence that action is being taken.
Time taken for resolution (ageing report)
Complaints must be resolved in a specific time so there is closure; if they drag on indefinitely, confidence in the whole system erodes.
Escalation mechanism
If the investor is dissatisfied, an independent party can review the complaint — the route upward beyond the entity's own system.

The grievance redress system of an Investment Adviser

SEBI has mandated a grievance-redressal system for Investment Advisers. Every IA's office must prominently display the details of the Compliance Officer and of the CEO / Partner / Proprietor — name, address, email and phone number — so clients know exactly whom to approach.

Approach the adviser first. The client raises the complaint with the IA, using the prominently displayed Compliance Officer / CEO contact details.
Escalate to SEBI via SCORES. If the client is not satisfied with the IA's response, they can lodge the grievance with SEBI through the SCORES system.

20.5

Capital market — SCORES and the ODR portal

In the capital market the investor first lodges the complaint directly with the market participant; if not satisfactorily redressed, it escalates through SCORES; after exhausting the options, the investor may initiate online dispute resolution (ODR) through the ODR portal.

SCORES is SEBI's online investor redressal mechanism for complaints related to all products and entities it regulates — companies that made share issues, mutual funds, PMS, VCFs, and intermediaries such as brokers, merchant bankers, depositories, RTAs, distributors and financial advisers. SEBI confirms the complaint is within its purview, forwards it to the entity, which must respond with an Action Taken Report (ATR) within the stipulated time; the investor can check status online.

SEBI does not deal with everything

SEBI does not deal with complaints against unlisted/delisted/wound-up/liquidated/sick companies, matters that are sub-judice, or complaints falling under the purview of other regulators. And the ODR/court route must be initiated within the law of limitation — reckoned from when the issue arose, the last transaction, or the disputed transaction, whichever is later.

20.6

Banking

The RBI set up the Banking Codes and Standards Board of India (BCSBI) as an independent, autonomous watchdog to ensure customers get fair treatment. It published the "Code of Banks' Commitments to Customers" — voluntarily adopted by most banks as their Fair Practice Code, setting minimum standards and benchmarks in customer service.

Within a bank, complaints climb a three-tier structure before the ombudsman; if still unresolved after a month, the customer approaches the Integrated Ombudsman.

Tier 1 — Branch Manager. The customer first takes the grievance to the branch.
Tier 2 — Zonal Manager. Unresolved complaints move up to the zonal level.
Tier 3 — GM (Customer Service). The final internal level of the bank's own system.
Integrated Ombudsman. If the bank does not give a satisfactory resolution within one month, the customer approaches the Banking (Integrated) Ombudsman, appointed by the RBI — who, if no settlement happens within one month, passes an award after hearing both sides.
Scope of the Integrated Ombudsman — and CPGRAMS

The Integrated Ombudsman Scheme covers receipt/payment of funds, interest charged or credited, penalty or fees, disputes on ATM/debit/credit cards (including cards issued by banks and NBFCs), and refusal of a loan without valid reasons. Separately, CPGRAMS — the Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (www.pgportal.gov.in) — forwards grievances to the concerned organisations, then monitors and reviews them.

20.7

Insurance

In insurance, a grievance is any communication expressing dissatisfaction about an action (or lack of action) regarding the standard/deficiency of service of an insurer/intermediary, or asking for remedial action. The buyer complains to the insurance company first; if unresolved, the Insurance Ombudsman — appointed by the Government of India under the Redressal of Public Grievance Rules, 1998 — steps in.

⚖️ Grounds for the Ombudsman

  • Partial or total repudiation of claims
  • Dispute about premium paid or payable
  • Dispute on the legal construction of policies re claims
  • Delay in settlement of claims
  • Non-issue of any insurance document after premium payment

⏱️ How it resolves

  • Recommendation within 1 month — if both parties, by mutual agreement, request mediation in writing
  • If that fails, a speaking Award within 3 months of receipt
  • Complainant conveys acceptance within 1 month as full & final settlement
  • Escalate to IRDAI via IGMS (Integrated Grievance Management System), tracked online
  • Still unhappy? Courts and the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 remain

20.8

Pension sector

The redress route for a pension grievance depends on who issued the scheme. Pension schemes issued by mutual funds follow SEBI's SCORES; those issued by insurance companies follow the IRDAI grievance redressal system. The National Pension System (NPS) has its own machinery.

Raise it with the CRA. For NPS, NSDL e-Governance Infrastructure Limited is the Central Recordkeeping Agency (CRA) appointed by PFRDA. The subscriber raises the grievance on the CRA's website; resolution is via call centre or written communication.
GRO at Mumbai. NPS operations are handled only through NSDL e-Gov's Head Office at Mumbai, where a Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) is appointed.
Escalate to the NPS Trust. If unresolved within 30 days, escalate to the NPS Trust (per PFRDA's Redressal of Subscriber Grievance Regulations, 2015), which follows up and responds within 30 days.
Appeal to the Ombudsman. If the NPS Trust does not resolve it within 30 days, or the subscriber is dissatisfied, they may prefer an appeal to the Ombudsman.

20.9

Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)

The Securities Appellate Tribunal hears appeals against orders in the securities space. Two sections of the SEBI Act define it. Under Section 15U, the SAT is not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure but is guided by the principles of natural justice and regulates its own procedure — while holding certain civil-court powers (summoning, discovery/production of documents, evidence on affidavit, commissions, review, dismissal for default, etc.). Any proceeding before it is a judicial proceeding within the meaning of the Indian Penal Code.

📜 Section 15T — Appeal to the SAT

  • Any person aggrieved by an order may appeal, where the order is of:
  • (i) the SEBI Board
  • (ii) an adjudicating officer under the SEBI Act
  • (iii) the IRDAI or the PFRDA
  • File within 45 days of receiving the order, in the specified form with the prescribed fee

⚖️ Section 15U — Procedure & powers

  • Not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure
  • Guided by the principles of natural justice; regulates its own procedure
  • Holds civil-court powers (summoning, discovery, evidence on affidavit, etc.)
  • A SAT proceeding is a judicial proceeding under the IPC
  • Appeal a SAT order to the Supreme Court within 60 days
