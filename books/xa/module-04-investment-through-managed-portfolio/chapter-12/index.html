Portfolio Manager

A Portfolio Management Service (PMS) hands a professional a basket of the client's assets to manage under a contract — aiming to minimise risk while maximising return with a customised solution. 12.1

Overview of portfolio managers in India

Risk and return are the two central aspects of investment. Portfolio management is about selecting and managing a basket of assets that minimises risk while maximising return, with the manager designing a customised solution for each client. A portfolio manager is a body corporate which, under a contract with a client, advises, directs or undertakes — as a discretionary manager or otherwise — the management or administration of a portfolio of securities or the client's funds.

📜 Regulated by SEBI

  • Registered & regulated under the SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020
  • PMS can be offered only by SEBI-registered entities
  • The first regulations were issued in January 1993 — even before the mutual fund regulations, showing the sector's importance to the regulator

🏢 Who provides PMS

  • Big brokerage firms
  • Asset management companies (AMCs)
  • Independent experts (boutique houses)

12.3

Structure of PMS in India

A portfolio manager must be a body corporate — a corporate entity that has a legal existence — which, under a contract or arrangement with a client, advises, directs or undertakes (as a discretionary manager or otherwise) the management or administration of a portfolio of securities or the client's funds.

PMS vs mutual fund — a segregated, personalised portfolio

Unlike a mutual fund, a PMS holds a personalised portfolio for each client, segregated in separate accounts. The minimum ticket size is ₹50 lakh, and a discretionary manager must manage each client's funds in a way that does not partake of the character of a mutual fund.

12.4

Registration requirements of a Portfolio Manager

Acting as a portfolio manager requires a certificate of registration from SEBI. The application is made to SEBI in Form A of Schedule I — a very detailed form — with a non-refundable fee. Form A covers the applicant's particulars, organisation structure, infrastructure, a three-year business plan, financial information, experience and declarations. Before granting the certificate, the regulator weighs several conditions:

👤 Principal Officer

  • Professional qualification in finance, law, accountancy or business management
  • At least five years' experience in related securities-market activities (portfolio manager, broker, IA, research analyst or fund manager)
  • The relevant NISM certification

👥 At least one more employee

  • Graduation from a recognised university/institution
  • At least two years' experience in related securities-market activities
  • (In addition to the Principal Officer and Compliance Officer)

12.5

Responsibilities of a Portfolio Manager

The SEBI Portfolio Managers Regulations place a series of duties on the manager — several protect client money directly. Tap through the key ones:

Guarding client money — the four safeguards

A manager must act in a fiduciary capacity, segregate each client's securities in separate accounts, keep all clients' funds in a separate account with a Scheduled Commercial Bank, and not accept less than ₹50 lakh. It may invest a maximum of 30% in its own associates/related parties — and only with a one-time written consent — and must not invest in below-investment-grade securities.

Costs, expenses & fees of investing in PMS

PMS charges come in two layers — a fixed cost paid regardless of outcome, and a performance-linked (profit-sharing) fee designed to reward good performance. Three ideas shape how the profit-sharing fee is computed. Tap each:

The high watermark, visualised

The high watermark is the value on which a profit-sharing fee was last paid. The fee only applies again once the portfolio crosses that previous high — so the manager is never paid twice for merely recovering lost ground:

Corpus starts at ₹50L and rises to ₹60L in Year 1 — a profit-sharing fee is charged and the high watermark steps up to ₹60L. In Year 2 the value slips to ₹55L: below the ₹60L high, so no fee. Only when the value crosses ₹60L again (Year 3, ₹62L) does the fee resume — on the new gains above the mark.

Catch-up vs no catch-up — same numbers, different fee

Take an 8% hurdle, a 20% profit-share and a ₹10 lakh gain over the high watermark. Under no catch-up, only the amount above the hurdle counts — 20% of (₹10L − ₹4L) = ₹1.20 lakh. Under catch-up, once the hurdle is beaten the fee applies to the full gain — 20% of ₹10L = ₹2 lakh. Returns are computed per financial year, and the fee applies only when the value tops the previous high watermark.

12.7

Direct access facility offered by PMS

Just as mutual funds offer a direct plan, a PMS must offer a direct access facility — a route with no distributor, and therefore lower cost. Both routes manage the money the same way; only the expenses differ.

🟢 Direct access facility

  • Investor approaches the PMS provider directly — no intermediary
  • Lower expenses → lower amount charged → higher net return
  • The fund-management process is identical to the regular route
  • The PMS must offer this facility to investors who want to skip intermediaries

🔵 Regular plan

  • Operates in the traditional way, through distributors
  • Carries normal expenses, including amounts paid to distributors
  • Same portfolio management — just a higher cost

The investment adviser's role is significant: first check whether the client actually needs a PMS versus other instruments; then, if the client is knowledgeable about the investment, recommend the direct access facility to save cost (performance is unaffected); and finally guide the client on whether a discretionary, non-discretionary or advisory service fits — helping raise the client's net return.
